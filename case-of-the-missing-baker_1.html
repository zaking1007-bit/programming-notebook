Pottawatomie County · Case No. 1895-B (Reopened)

The Missing Baker
of Main Street

A self-guided investigation for two detectives — tonight only, county lines optional

The situation: Barnaby Voss ran the little ice cream counter on Main Street for forty years. Tonight was supposed to be his retirement party. He never showed. The shop was left running, the lights on, a fresh batch in the case — and Barnaby nowhere to be found. What he left behind is a trail, and this time it doesn't stay inside city limits. No answer is handed to you outright — you'll have to reason each one out, and you may need to look a few things up.

Stop One · The Scene

1895 Homemade Ice Cream

102 E Main St, Shawnee · open in Maps

Start here. Order whatever you want — this is the last batch Barnaby ever made, according to the note taped inside the door. Sit down, take a bite, and read what he left behind.

"If you're reading this, I've gone looking for the county's oldest names before I start my own second act. Forty years scooping in this county, and I never once thought about who it's all named after. There's a town just south of here that borrows its name straight from a Shawnee leader — the one who spent his life trying to hold this land together before it came apart. Go stand on his street."

Riddle: What town, just south of Shawnee, is named for a Shawnee leader who tried to unite tribes against westward settlement in the early 1800s?

Need a hint?
The town shares its name with the leader — and with the tribe Shawnee itself is named for.

Correct — Tecumseh. Barnaby's note continues: "Named for the Shawnee leader himself. Go stand on the old main drag — Broadway. There's a row of storefronts there older than I am."

→ Drive to S Broadway St, Tecumseh (about 10 minutes) for Stop Two.

Stop Two · Main Street, Two Towns Over

Tecumseh Historic Broadway District

114 S Broadway St, Tecumseh · open in Maps

Park and walk the block. Everything's closed for the night, but the storefronts are still worth reading — especially the antique shop that sells baked goods on the side. Barnaby's next note was tucked in the doorframe.

"I've spent my whole life two towns wide, never further. There's a park back toward Shawnee where the state keeps a wound it still tends every April — grass and a memorial, and inside it, something green that has no business surviving an Oklahoma winter. It isn't native. It isn't supposed to be here. Neither was I, most nights, after close. Find the plant that doesn't belong, and you'll find where I went next."

Riddle: Name the out-of-place plant, and the Shawnee park and memorial where it grows.

Need a hint?
Think of the plant most associated with pandas, not Oklahoma. The memorial marks a 1995 bombing about 40 miles from here.

Correct — bamboo, at Sister City Park. It grows near the walking trail by the Shawnee airport, beside the OKC bombing memorial. Barnaby's note: "Walk the loop. Find the quiet. That's where I'm going after this."

→ Drive back toward Shawnee, to Sister City Park, N Airport Dr (about 15 minutes from here) for Stop Three.

Stop Three · The Getaway

Sister City Park

1600 N Airport Dr, Shawnee · open in Maps

Walk a stretch of the trail together. Find a bench, or just stand under the bamboo, and read the last page Barnaby left.

"I used to close the shop every night at nine and look east, past the rooftops, at the one structure in this town tall enough to still catch the last light. It doesn't ring for a fire or a ship coming in. It rings for a Baptist school on a hill, over a quad I never once set foot on in forty years. Its name matches the chapel it stands beside. Meet me there, under it, and we'll call this closed."

Riddle: What structure is Barnaby describing, and where does it stand?

Need a hint?
It's on OBU's campus, and its name is also the name of the chapel it sits beside.

Correct — Raley Chapel Clock Tower. One last drive.

→ Head to 2501 Raley Dr (OBU campus) for the final stop, about 10 minutes away.

Stop Four · Case Closed

Raley Chapel Clock Tower, OBU

2501 Raley Dr, Shawnee · open in Maps

Park near the quad and look up at the tower, lit against the dark. There's no more searching left — just one thing to decide.

"You found every place I loved in this town without ever needing a badge. Truth is, nobody took me. I just finally asked myself what forty years behind a counter was for, and the answer was: to open a bigger one. There's a second shop going in across town, and I wanted the two of you — whoever you are, my last customers of the night — to be the first to know before anyone else. Case closed. Go finish your ice cream before it melts."
